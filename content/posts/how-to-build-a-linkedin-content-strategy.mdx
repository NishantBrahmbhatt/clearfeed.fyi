A strategy is not a calendar full of squares.

It is an answer, in plain language, to **who you are talking to**, **what pain you help with**, and **what you do between posts** so the feed does not eat the relationship work.

Skip the first two and you get tactics without traction.

## What is the foundation of an effective LinkedIn content strategy?

One sentence of positioning.

Not “thought leader in marketing.” Something a tired professional could repeat to a friend: who you help, what outcome you move.

Specificity self-selects the audience. Generic posts attract generic engagement. Tight positioning attracts people who might actually hire you, refer you, or learn from you on purpose.

Everything else, topics, tone, who you comment under, flows from that sentence.

## How do you choose what topics to post about on LinkedIn?

Steal from your week.

**Questions you get twice.** If two clients asked it, five hundred strangers are wondering.

**Hard lessons.** “What I misunderstood about X until I lived it” beats “five tips for X” most Tuesdays.

**Real disagreements.** If you believe something that your industry says loudly in the other direction, say it carefully and with receipts.

Keep a note. Add lines in meetings, in Slack, on the train. If the list is empty, you are not paying attention to your job, not your “creativity.”

## What does a realistic LinkedIn content calendar look like?

**Two or three posts a week** is enough to compound without swallowing your life <Citation source="Buffer" href="https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/" year="2025" />.

**One heavier piece** when you have a framework, a story with numbers, or a teardown.

**One or two lighter posts** when you have a single sharp observation.

**Fifteen minutes of comments a day**, every workday. Non-negotiable if growth is the goal; posting without engaging is half a strategy.

## How do you write LinkedIn posts that generate engagement?

**Line one earns the tap.** Everything after “see more” is irrelevant if line one is mush.

**Details beat vibes.** Numbers, proper nouns, timelines.

**Air on the page.** Phone screens reward short paragraphs.

**Have a spine.** Mild, honest opinions invite replies. Wallpaper does not.

**Ask a question someone could answer in a paragraph**, not “thoughts?”

## How do you measure whether your LinkedIn content strategy is working?

**First ninety days:** average impressions per post trending up, even slowly, beats obsessing over one banger.

**After that:** follower slope month to month.

**Always:** are comments getting smarter? If yes, you are reaching the right room.

Single posts lie. Twenty-post patterns tell the truth.
