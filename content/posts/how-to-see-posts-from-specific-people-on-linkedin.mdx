LinkedIn does not promise you a complete view of your connections. It promises you a **ranked** view: whatever the model thinks will spike engagement, which is not the same as “people I chose.”

When someone you care about goes quiet in your feed, they might have gone quiet in life. More often, the algorithm simply found something louder.

## Why doesn't LinkedIn show posts from people you follow?

Three mechanics, one outcome: your favourites get crowded out.

**Engagement velocity.** Early likes and comments act like jet fuel <Citation source="Hootsuite" href="https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/" year="2025" />. Posts that spark fast travel; posts that do not, stall, even among connections <Citation source="AuthoredUp" href="https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm" year="2025" />.

**Second-degree noise.** Your network touches a viral post; LinkedIn shows you that post. You never followed the author. Your actual connections slide down the deck.

**Format bias.** Carousels, polls, video often get more oxygen than plain text. If your people post in “quiet” formats, the feed can bury them without malice, just math.

## What are the other ways to see specific people's posts on LinkedIn?

Native options exist. They just scale badly.

**Profile → Posts.** Perfect for one human. Awkward for ten.

**Bell notifications.** You get pings, not a single calm queue.

**Following tab** (`linkedin.com/feed/following`). Narrower than home. Still not “only these twelve, in order.”

For a real roster, you want a roster tool.
