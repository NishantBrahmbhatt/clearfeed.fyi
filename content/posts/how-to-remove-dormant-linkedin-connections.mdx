A cluttered LinkedIn connection list is not just an aesthetic problem. It actively dilutes the reach of everything you post. LinkedIn's algorithm distributes your content to a portion of your network — if that network is full of dormant accounts, irrelevant connections, and people who have not logged in for years, a significant share of your post's distribution is wasted on people who will never see it. Cleaning your connection list is one of the most overlooked ways to improve your LinkedIn performance.

Here is exactly how to do it safely.

## Why removing dormant LinkedIn connections actually matters

Most LinkedIn advice focuses on growing your network. Fewer people talk about pruning it — but the case for regular cleanup is strong.

LinkedIn has a hard limit of 30,000 first-degree connections.  Once you hit it, the Follow button replaces the Connect button on your profile and you cannot accept new connection requests until you remove existing ones. Most users are nowhere near this limit, but it illustrates the point — connection quality matters more than connection quantity.

More practically, LinkedIn's algorithm distributes your posts to a sample of your connections in the first hour after publishing. If your acceptance rate for connection requests drops below 30%, LinkedIn reduces your weekly outreach limits  — which means a network full of people who never engage with you can signal low quality to the algorithm over time. Dormant connections drag down your engagement rates, which affects how widely your content gets distributed.

The cleaner your network, the higher the proportion of active, relevant people seeing your posts. That is the real reason to do this.

## Does LinkedIn notify people when you remove them?

No. LinkedIn does not notify users when they are removed from someone's connections list.  The removal is silent. The person will only notice if they visit your profile and see the Connect button where Message used to be.

One thing worth knowing: removing a connection also withdraws any recommendations or endorsements you have exchanged with that person. If you have a genuine professional recommendation from someone, removing them deletes it permanently. Check before removing anyone you have exchanged recommendations with.

## The safest daily limit for removing connections

LinkedIn does not publish official daily removal limits. Community consensus from LinkedIn power users and automation platforms consistently suggests that 10 to 30 removals per day is safe for most accounts. Removing more than 50 per day — especially in one sitting — risks triggering LinkedIn's anti-spam systems.

The key is to spread removals over multiple days and mix them with normal LinkedIn activity — posting, commenting, responding to messages. An account that suddenly removes 200 connections in an hour with no other activity looks like a bot. An account that removes 20 connections over the course of a normal LinkedIn session looks human.

## How to remove connections manually on LinkedIn (no tools required)

LinkedIn's native approach is the safest because it involves no third-party tools and no automation risk. It is slower but completely within LinkedIn's terms of service.

**Step 1.** Go to linkedin.com/mynetwork/invite-connect/connections/

**Step 2.** Use the search and filter options to narrow your connections list. Filter by industry, location, company, or job title to find categories of connections that are no longer relevant.

**Step 3.** Click the three-dot menu next to any connection and select Remove connection.

**Step 4.** Limit yourself to 20 to 30 removals per session. Do not do them all at once.

This method works well for removing up to a few hundred connections over a few weeks. For larger cleanups, a tool makes the process significantly faster.

## What about before removing — should you unfollow instead?

This depends on what you are trying to achieve.

**Unfollow** if you want to clean your feed without changing your network. Unfollowing removes someone's posts from your timeline but keeps the connection intact. They can still message you, you remain first-degree connections, and recommendations stay in place. ## The right order of operations for a LinkedIn connection cleanup

Do not approach this as a one-time mass deletion. Do it methodically over a few weeks:

**Week 1 — audit before removing.** Export your connections as a CSV from LinkedIn Settings → Data Privacy → Get a copy of your data. Review the list and identify categories of connections that are no longer relevant. This gives you a clear picture of what you are working with before you start removing anyone.

**Week 2 onwards — remove in small batches.** Work through your list category by category, removing 15 to 20 connections per day maximum. Filter by company, location, or industry to find natural clusters of irrelevant connections. Combine removals with normal LinkedIn activity in the same session.
