You open LinkedIn expecting to see updates from your connections and the creators you follow. Instead, you get a flood of viral posts from people you've never heard of, engagement bait from accounts you'd never choose to follow, and suggested content from LinkedIn's algorithm.

This isn't random. LinkedIn is deliberately doing this to you — and once you understand why, you can stop it.

## Why LinkedIn shows you content from strangers

LinkedIn's feed algorithm does not simply show you posts from your connections in chronological order. It is designed to maximise time on the platform, which means surfacing content it predicts you will engage with — regardless of whether you follow the person who posted it.

There are four main reasons your feed fills up with irrelevant posts:

**1. Second and third-degree connections**
When someone in your network likes or comments on a post, LinkedIn surfaces that post to you. This means any viral post touching your extended network can land in your feed, even if you have zero relationship with the original author.

**2. "Suggested" posts based on topics**
LinkedIn infers what topics interest you from your profile, past engagement, and job title. It then injects posts on those topics whether or not you follow the creator. The more you engage with any post — even to dismiss it — the more it assumes you want similar content.

**3. Sponsored content and ads**
A portion of your feed is always paid placement. These are not from people you follow.

**4. Content from Groups you joined**
If you joined LinkedIn Groups, their activity bleeds into your main feed by default.

## The real problem: you have no control over the default feed

LinkedIn does not offer a native way to filter your feed to show only specific people. There is no chronological mode, no "following only" toggle like Twitter/X introduced. The algorithm is the product — removing it would reduce the engagement metrics LinkedIn reports to advertisers.

This means fixing your feed requires going outside LinkedIn's default settings.

## What LinkedIn actually wants vs what you want

It's worth being clear-eyed about this. LinkedIn's business model depends on keeping you scrolling. The algorithm is not broken — it is working exactly as LinkedIn designed it. Every irrelevant post is a bet that you might engage with it, and every engagement is data LinkedIn monetises.

You wanting to see only content from specific people is at direct odds with what LinkedIn the company wants from your session.

The fix is not to try to game the algorithm. The fix is to bypass the feed entirely using a tool that gives you the control LinkedIn withholds.
