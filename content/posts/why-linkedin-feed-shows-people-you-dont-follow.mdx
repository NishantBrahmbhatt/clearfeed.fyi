Your feed is not a list of people you picked.

It is a bet. LinkedIn's algorithm is betting it knows what will keep you scrolling longer than your own follow list would, and once you see the incentive behind that bet, the strangers in your timeline stop feeling random. They start feeling inevitable.

## Why does LinkedIn show you posts from people you don't follow?

Because the feed is tuned for **time on platform**, not for your preferences.

LinkedIn pulled in over $15 billion in revenue in 2023 <Citation source="LinkedIn Newsroom" href="https://news.linkedin.com/2023/july/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-fy23-q4-earnings" year="2023" />, mostly ads and premium subs, and both get better when you stay. Longer sessions mean more inventory, more impressions, more of everything that shows up on a quarterly slide.

So the algorithm has a job: surface stuff that sparks engagement, full stop. Your follows matter. They are not the main character.

## What are the specific reasons strangers appear in your LinkedIn feed?

Four levers. Same feed. Different ways in.

**Second- and third-degree amplification.** Someone you know touches a post (like, comment, repost), and LinkedIn pushes that post toward you <Citation source="Hootsuite" href="https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/" year="2025" />. You might never have heard of the author. Does not matter. The network did the introduction.

**Topic suggestions.** LinkedIn reads your title, your keywords, what you linger on, what your industry peers click. Then it drops in posts on those themes from accounts you never followed. Engage with something, even to argue with it, and the machine often hears "more of this shape, please."

**Sponsored slots.** Paid. Targeted. Not optional. Your follow graph is almost irrelevant there.

**Group bleed.** Joined a group? Its activity can wash into the main timeline. People you have never connected with start looking like part of your "normal" day.

## Why can't you just turn off the algorithm on LinkedIn?

You cannot flip it off.

A pure, chronological, follows-only river would probably **lower** the engagement numbers LinkedIn sells to advertisers, and the ranking layer is not a cute setting you can disable. It is part of the product they monetise.

So you are not fighting a toggle. You are fighting incentives. Native tools can trim the noise. They do not rewrite the contract.

## What are the other ways to reduce unwanted content in your LinkedIn feed?

LinkedIn gives you half-measures. Useful half-measures.

**Unfollow hard.** You can unfollow without disconnecting. They do not get a notification. Your graph stays polite; your feed gets quieter. Slow work. It adds up.

**The Following feed.** `linkedin.com/feed/following` narrows the firehose. Algorithm still in the loop. Still not "only these twelve humans, in order."

**Hide and train.** Three dots, "not interested," repeat. It nudges the model over weeks. It is not a cure.

None of that is the same as owning the roster.

## Does LinkedIn's Following feed actually solve the problem?

Partly.

You will see fewer strangers. You will not see **zero** algorithmic drift. Followed accounts still get ranked, not strictly clock-ordered, and suggestions can slip through.

For a lot of people, that is enough to breathe. It is still not the thing people say they want when they complain at 11 p.m. about their feed: a clean, reliable stream from a defined set of humans, newest first. For that, you leave the native feed as entertainment and wire something else for work.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my LinkedIn feed show people I don't follow?**
The platform optimises for time on site. That means surfacing high-engagement posts from your extended network, topic suggestions matched to your profile, and sponsored placements, even when you never followed the author.

**How do I stop seeing posts from people I don't follow on LinkedIn?**
Swap the main feed for a curated list. [MyFeedIn](https://myfeedin.co?atp=clearfeed) does that inside Chrome. Unfollowing connections you still want as connections (without disconnecting) also shrinks second-degree noise over time.

**Does LinkedIn have a following-only feed?**
There is a narrower view at linkedin.com/feed/following. It is not a pure "only these people, in order" mode; the algorithm still ranks and can suggest.

**Why does LinkedIn show viral posts from strangers in my feed?**
Velocity beats your follow graph. When people you are linked to touch a post early, LinkedIn tests it on you too, whether you know the poster or not.

**Can I make LinkedIn show only posts from my connections?**
No native filter does that cleanly. Extensions such as MyFeedIn are how you pin a feed to a defined set of profiles today.

**Is there a way to control what appears in my LinkedIn feed?**
Yes. Strongest lever: [MyFeedIn](https://myfeedin.co?atp=clearfeed) custom feeds. Supporting moves: unfollow, Following tab, hide and train the algorithm.
