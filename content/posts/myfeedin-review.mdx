MyFeedIn is a Chrome extension built to solve one of LinkedIn's most persistent problems, the algorithm deciding what you see. I've used it daily for several months. Here's an honest take on what it does well, where it falls short, and who should actually pay for it.

## What is MyFeedIn and what does it do?

MyFeedIn replaces LinkedIn's algorithm-controlled feed with custom feeds you build yourself. You create named lists of specific LinkedIn users (prospects, industry peers, creators you want to engage with), and MyFeedIn shows only their posts in a clean, distraction-free interface directly inside LinkedIn.

Beyond custom feeds, the current version includes post analytics for your own content (views, likes, comments, engagement rate), comment analytics that show which comments drove the most profile visits, and a focus mode that blocks LinkedIn's main feed so you don't accidentally fall into it.

The tool is built for a specific type of user, someone who wants to use LinkedIn intentionally for relationship-building or business development, not passive content consumption.

## What does MyFeedIn do well?

**Custom feeds work exactly as advertised.** Add people to a feed, and you see their posts. Nothing else. The interface is clean and fast, posts load quickly, and you can comment, like, and react directly from the feed without getting pushed back to LinkedIn's main timeline. It's the core feature, and it's genuinely well executed.

**The free plan is a real product.** Lots of tools use free tiers as crippled demos designed to frustrate you into upgrading. MyFeedIn's free plan (one feed, up to 10 people) is genuinely useful. If you're a solo LinkedIn user who wants consistent engagement with a targeted group, the free plan can be enough indefinitely.

**Setup is fast and frictionless.** From installing the extension to populating your first feed takes under two minutes. No complex onboarding. No data import. No account linking beyond logging into LinkedIn like you normally do.

**It does not touch your LinkedIn account.** MyFeedIn reads post data from LinkedIn's public interface to build feeds, but it doesn't perform actions on your account. It can't send invites or automate engagement. Because of that, it isn't doing the kind of behavior LinkedIn typically penalizes.

## What are MyFeedIn's limitations?

**Desktop only.** MyFeedIn is a browser extension, so there is no mobile app. If you mostly use LinkedIn on your phone (which many people do), MyFeedIn won't help much. That's the biggest limitation, and it's worth being clear about before you set it up.

**Dependent on LinkedIn's interface.** MyFeedIn reads LinkedIn's web interface, so major site changes on LinkedIn can temporarily break parts of the extension. That's a risk with any browser extension built on top of a third-party platform. Historically, the team ships fixes quickly when this happens, but you should know the dependency exists.

**The analytics are useful but not deep.** You get core metrics (views, likes, comments, engagement rate), but not the depth of a dedicated LinkedIn analytics product like Shield. For most users, built-in analytics are enough. If deep performance analysis is a priority, a specialist tool may be the better fit.

## Who is MyFeedIn for?

MyFeedIn is best suited for three types of LinkedIn users:

**Creators who post regularly and want to build relationships through commenting.** Consistent engagement with a targeted list is one of the strongest LinkedIn growth strategies. MyFeedIn makes that workflow systematic and time efficient.

**Sales and business development professionals.** Building a custom feed of prospects and monitoring their posts for engagement opportunities is a practical social selling workflow. On paid plans, shared lists extend that workflow to teams.

**Professionals who waste time on LinkedIn but can't quit it.** If LinkedIn is necessary for your work but you regularly lose 45 minutes to unintentional scrolling, MyFeedIn's focus mode and custom feeds remove the triggers that cause that behavior.

MyFeedIn isn't useful for passive LinkedIn users who don't engage regularly, or for anyone who mostly uses LinkedIn on mobile.

## Is the paid plan worth it?

The free plan covers the core use case (one custom feed, up to 10 people, unlimited use). For most individual users, that's enough.

The paid plan makes sense in two specific situations: you want to manage multiple separate feeds (for example prospects, peers, and creators at the same time), or you're using LinkedIn in a team workflow and want to share curated lists with colleagues.

If neither applies, start with the free plan. You don't need to upgrade to get real value.
