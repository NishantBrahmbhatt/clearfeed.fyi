LinkedIn's feed is engineered to maximise your time on the platform, not your productivity. The average LinkedIn session lasts 14 minutes and 20 seconds <Citation source="Backlinko" href="https://backlinko.com/linkedin-users" year="2025" /> — and for most professionals, a significant portion of that is unintentional scrolling that produces no meaningful networking, visibility, or business value. The fix is not willpower. It is a system that removes the feed entirely.

## Why is LinkedIn so addictive and hard to use efficiently?

LinkedIn is addictive because it uses the same infinite scroll mechanics as social media platforms designed for entertainment. Every element — the algorithm-sorted feed, the notification badge, the suggested posts — is optimised to extend your session, because longer sessions mean more ad impressions and more revenue for LinkedIn.

This is not a flaw. It is the product working as designed. LinkedIn generated over $15 billion in revenue in fiscal year 2023 <Citation source="LinkedIn Newsroom" href="https://news.linkedin.com/2023/july/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-fy23-q4-earnings" year="2023" />, almost entirely from advertising and premium subscriptions that depend on time on platform. You're not bad at managing your time on LinkedIn. You're fighting a product with hundreds of engineers whose job is to keep you there.

Understanding this reframes the problem. The solution is structural, not behavioural.

## The 15-minute LinkedIn system that actually works

A focused 15-minute LinkedIn session produces more relationship-building value than an hour of passive scrolling. Here is the exact system:

**Step 1 — Build a custom feed (once, takes 2 minutes)**
Install MyFeedIn, create one feed, add 10 people you genuinely want to engage with. This is the only setup step and you never repeat it.

**Step 2 — Set a timer before opening LinkedIn**
Set 10 to 15 minutes on your phone before opening the tab. When it goes off, you close LinkedIn. The timer removes the decision of when you've done enough.

**Step 3 — Do exactly three things per session**
Open your custom feed. Leave two or three genuine comments on posts from people you want to build a relationship with. Check notifications for direct replies only.

**Step 4 — Turn off all notifications except direct messages**
Go to LinkedIn Settings then Notifications and disable everything except direct messages and connection requests. Every other notification is an invitation to open the feed.

**Step 5 — Write posts outside LinkedIn**
Draft posts in a notes app or Google Doc. Paste, publish, close the tab. Never write directly in LinkedIn's composer — it keeps you in the platform longer than necessary.

## Does the 15-minute LinkedIn routine actually work for growing your network?

Yes. Consistent focused engagement outperforms sporadic long sessions for LinkedIn growth. LinkedIn's algorithm rewards regular engagement over volume <Citation source="Hootsuite" href="https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/" year="2025" /> — commenting genuinely on three posts daily is more effective for visibility than spending two hours on the platform once a week.

The people who grow fastest on LinkedIn are not the ones spending the most time there. They're the ones who show up consistently with brief, intentional engagement, then leave. A custom feed makes this possible by removing the friction of finding relevant posts to engage with.

## How do you avoid getting pulled back into the LinkedIn feed?

Three structural changes prevent the scroll habit from reasserting itself:

**Replace the habit trigger.** The moment you open LinkedIn and see the main feed, the scroll habit activates. MyFeedIn changes what you see when you open LinkedIn — your custom feed loads instead of the algorithm's feed, removing the trigger at the source.

**Unfollow aggressively.** You can unfollow connections without disconnecting from them. Unfollow anyone whose posts you don't actively want to see. A smaller follow list means the algorithm has less surface area to fill with irrelevant content.

**Use LinkedIn on desktop only.** The mobile app is optimised for casual habitual checking. Using LinkedIn only on desktop creates a natural friction point that reduces impulsive opens. Research shows 69% of LinkedIn traffic comes from desktop <Citation source="DemandSage" href="https://www.demandsage.com/linkedin-statistics/" year="2026" />, confirming most serious LinkedIn activity happens there anyway.
