The average LinkedIn session runs about fourteen minutes <Citation source="Backlinko" href="https://backlinko.com/linkedin-users" year="2025" />. For a lot of us, a big slice of that is not networking. It is drift.

Not because you are lazy. Because the product is tuned to keep you inside the tab.

## Why is LinkedIn so addictive and hard to use efficiently?

Infinite scroll. Badges. Suggested posts. Same playbook as entertainment apps, dressed in a blazer.

LinkedIn booked huge revenue in 2023 <Citation source="LinkedIn Newsroom" href="https://news.linkedin.com/2023/july/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-fy23-q4-earnings" year="2023" />, almost all of it from ads and subscriptions that get stronger when **time on site** goes up. You are not in a fair fight with your calendar. You are in a fair fight with a team whose KPI is “one more minute.”

That is useful to admit. Once you do, “try harder” stops sounding like advice. **Structure** starts sounding like one.

## The 15-minute LinkedIn system that actually works

Fifteen focused minutes beats an hour of “I will just check.” Here is the loop.

**Step 1 — Build the list once.** Install MyFeedIn, name a feed, add up to ten people you really want in your corner. Done. You are not rebuilding this daily.

**Step 2 — Timer first.** Ten or fifteen minutes on the clock **before** the tab opens. When it buzzes, you leave. The clock ends the negotiation.

**Step 3 — Three moves.** Scan your custom feed. Drop two or three comments that would still make sense if your name were not attached. Peek notifications for **direct** replies only. That is the session.

**Step 4 — Silence the bait.** In Settings → Notifications, turn off everything except DMs and connection requests. Everything else is a trapdoor back into the feed.

**Step 5 — Write elsewhere.** Draft in Notes or Docs. Paste, post, close. The in-app composer is a lobby designed to keep you shopping.

## Does the 15-minute LinkedIn routine actually work for growing your network?

Yes.

The algorithm likes **steady** engagement more than heroic one-off marathons <Citation source="Hootsuite" href="https://blog.hootsuite.com/linkedin-algorithm/" year="2025" />. Three real comments today and tomorrow beats a guilt binge on Sunday.

The people who grow are rarely the ones living in the tab. They are the ones who show up, do the small thing, log off. A custom feed makes that possible because you are not panning for gold in sludge.

## How do you avoid getting pulled back into the LinkedIn feed?

Three guardrails:

**Change the first screen.** If opening LinkedIn always means the algorithm feed, your brain learns “scroll now.” MyFeedIn changes the entry point so the habit never arms.

**Unfollow like you mean it.** Connections can stay. Noise can go. They do not get a notification. Your feed shrinks; the model has less junk to weaponise.

**Desktop discipline.** The phone app wants little checks all day. Desktop adds friction. Most serious work traffic is desktop anyway <Citation source="DemandSage" href="https://www.demandsage.com/linkedin-statistics/" year="2026" />.
