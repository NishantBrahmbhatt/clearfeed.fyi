LinkedIn's feed is engineered to maximize your time on the platform, not your productivity. ## Why is LinkedIn so addictive and hard to use efficiently?

LinkedIn is addictive because it uses the same infinite scroll mechanics as entertainment-first social platforms. Every element, the algorithm-sorted feed, the notification badge, the suggested posts, is tuned to extend your session since longer sessions create more ad impressions and more revenue for LinkedIn.

It's not a flaw. It's the product doing exactly what it was built to do. LinkedIn generated over $15 billion in revenue in 2023, mostly from advertising and premium subscriptions tied directly to time on platform. You're not bad at managing your time on LinkedIn. You're up against a product built by hundreds of engineers whose job is to keep you there.

Once you look at it that way, the problem changes shape. You need a structural fix, not a behavioral pep talk.

## The 15-minute LinkedIn system that actually works

A focused 15-minute LinkedIn session creates more relationship value than two hours of passive scrolling. Here's the exact system:

**Step 1 — Build a custom feed (once, takes 2 minutes)**
Install MyFeedIn, create one feed, and add 10 people you genuinely want to engage with. You do this setup once, then you're done.

**Step 2 — Set a timer before opening LinkedIn**
Set a 10 to 15 minute timer on your phone before opening the tab. When it goes off, close LinkedIn. No debate. The timer removes the constant decision about whether you've done enough.

**Step 3 — Do exactly three things per session**
Open your custom feed. Leave two or three genuine comments on posts from people you want to build a relationship with. Check notifications for direct replies only.

**Step 4 — Turn off all notifications except direct messages**
Go to LinkedIn Settings → Notifications and disable everything except direct messages and connection requests. Every other notification is an invitation to open the feed.

**Step 5 — Write posts outside LinkedIn**
Draft posts in a notes app or Google Doc. Paste, publish, close the tab. Don't write directly in LinkedIn's composer, it keeps you on the platform longer than necessary.

## Does the 15-minute LinkedIn routine actually work for growing your network?

Yes. Consistent, focused engagement beats sporadic long sessions for LinkedIn growth. The algorithm rewards regular engagement over volume, so commenting genuinely on three posts each day is often more effective than spending two hours on the platform once a week.

The people who grow fastest on LinkedIn usually aren't the ones spending the most time there. They're the ones who show up consistently, engage with intent, then leave. A custom feed makes that possible because it removes the friction of finding the right posts to engage with.

## How do you avoid getting pulled back into the LinkedIn feed?

Three structural changes keep the scroll habit from creeping back:

**Replace the habit trigger.** The moment you open LinkedIn and see the main feed, the scroll habit kicks in. MyFeedIn changes what appears when you open LinkedIn, your custom feed loads instead of the algorithm feed, so the trigger disappears at the source.

**Unfollow aggressively.** You can unfollow connections without disconnecting from them. Unfollow anyone whose posts you don't actively want to see. A smaller follow list gives the algorithm less surface area to fill with irrelevant content.

**Use LinkedIn on desktop only.** The mobile app is optimized for quick habitual checking. Using LinkedIn only on desktop adds natural friction and cuts down impulsive opens throughout the day.
